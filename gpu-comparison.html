How this tool works

The SystemFit PC GPU Comparison Tool organizes graphics cards by their published specifications and broad performance class so you can compare the parts that are actually relevant to a gaming or PC-building decision. Depending on the cards selected, useful differences include VRAM capacity, memory type, bus width, power requirements, performance tier, ray-tracing capability and intended workload. The tool is designed to make those trade-offs easier to see without pretending that one universal score can describe every GPU.

Start by comparing cards that fit your budget, case, power supply and target resolution. Then look at the specifications that matter for your workload instead of choosing a card solely because one number is larger. A GPU with more VRAM, for example, is not automatically faster than a stronger GPU with less memory.

How to read the result

Use the comparison to narrow your choices, not to replace game-specific testing. Performance class is useful for a quick first pass, while VRAM and power requirements help determine whether a card is practical for your system. Consider the resolution you actually plan to play at: a card that is excellent for 1080p may not be the right choice for demanding 4K workloads.

Also check the physical and electrical requirements before buying. Partner models can have different cooler sizes, clocks, power connectors and dimensions even when they use the same GPU. If you are unsure about your system balance, try the Bottleneck Calculator and read the VRAM guide before making a final choice.

Limits of this estimate

This comparison does not produce a universal FPS guarantee or account for every game, driver version, CPU combination, cooling condition or partner-card design. Published specifications can also vary between reference designs and individual models. Performance can change substantially with resolution, ray tracing, upscaling, frame generation and game engine. Treat the broad performance class as a comparison aid and confirm the exact card's specifications, dimensions and PSU requirements with the manufacturer before purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Does the highest-tier GPU always win?

Not for every workload. Game engine, resolution, settings and CPU limits can change the practical result.

Is more VRAM always better?

More VRAM can help demanding workloads, but it does not automatically make a GPU faster. The GPU's overall performance still matters.

Can two versions of the same GPU perform differently?

Yes. Partner cards can use different cooling, power limits and factory clocks, although they share the same underlying GPU.

How much should power consumption influence my choice?

It matters for PSU capacity, connectors, heat and system design. Always verify the exact model's requirements before buying.

What should I check after choosing a GPU?

Use the PC Build Planner for compatibility and the VRAM guide for memory capacity context.
