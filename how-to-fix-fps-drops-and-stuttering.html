How to Fix FPS Drops & Stuttering on PC

A practical troubleshooting path for sudden FPS drops, inconsistent frame times, shader stutter, thermal throttling and background-system problems.

Updated October 2026 · SystemFit PC

Quick answer: First determine whether the problem is low average FPS or . Check GPU/CPU usage, temperatures, VRAM/RAM pressure and frame-time behavior before changing ten settings at once.

FPS and stutter are not the same problem

A game can average 100 FPS and still feel bad if some frames take much longer to arrive. Average FPS describes throughput; frame time and 1% lows help reveal consistency. That distinction is important when troubleshooting hitching.

Common causes of sudden stutter

Shader compilation

Some games compile shaders during play or after an update. The result can be short but noticeable hitches even when average FPS looks healthy.

Thermal throttling

If clocks fall after the system heats up, performance can deteriorate over a session. Check temperatures and clock behavior rather than assuming the game is at fault.

Background software

Browsers, overlays, recording tools, launchers and updates can compete for CPU, memory or storage resources. Test with unnecessary background workloads closed.

Storage and asset streaming

Modern open-world games can continuously stream assets. An old hard drive can become a weak point for loading and traversal behavior even though it does not directly determine the normal rendered FPS.

Don't start by reinstalling Windows

A Windows reinstall is a last resort, not a universal FPS fix. Measure first. If the problem began after a driver, game update or hardware change, investigate that event before wiping a working system.

Best troubleshooting habit: change one variable, reproduce the problem, and compare the result. Random tweaks make it harder to identify the real cause.

When the bottleneck is the real problem

If your FPS is consistently limited by one component, no amount of Windows cleanup will create a large performance jump. Use the Bottleneck Calculator to get a structured starting point, then use the FPS Calculator to model your target scenario.

Frequently Asked Questions

Heat buildup can cause CPU or GPU clocks to fall. Background processes, memory pressure or a game-specific leak can also worsen over time.
